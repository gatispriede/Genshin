Genius Invokation — getting cards, and a Skirk deck

Checked 27 Oct 2026, TCG version 6.3-era card pool.

I can't give you a working sharing code. The code packs 33 internal card IDs into base64, and I don't have a verified ID table — a guessed code either fails to import or imports the wrong deck. I decoded the structure of the one you sent (51 bytes, 33 twelve-bit slots: 3 characters + 30 action cards) but not which card each ID is. Everything below is a build-by-hand list instead.

1 · How you get character cards

1
The tutorial hands you four Diluc, Kaeya, Sucrose, Fischl — free from the world quest Battlefield of Dice, Cats, and Cards.
2
Everything else comes from the Invitation Board — this is the main route Wall-mounted board in The Cat's Tail. Spend a Match Invitation Letter to unlock a character's Invitational, beat them on Friendly Fracas, and their card is the reward. Once unlocked you can rematch freely until you've claimed every objective.
3
Talent cards come from the same duel Clearing all of that character's challenge objectives on Friendly Fracas gives their Talent card. You can't buy Talent cards.
4
Player Level rewards Levelling your TCG Player Level pays out cards and more Match Invitation Letters.
✗
You cannot buy character cards Prince's shop sells Action cards, letters, card backs, boxes and skins — not character cards. The only way to a character card is to beat them.

Three of these solve each other. Open-world duels pay the Lucky Coins and tick off objective 2 and push your Player Level to 2 — which is what unlocks both the Tavern Guest Challenge and the ability to buy the letter in the first place.
1
Do a lap of Mondstadt's eight duellists They're all in or at the city. Each duel has a win objective plus bonus objectives (round limits, no characters lost), so 20 objectives comes much faster than 20 duels — you're 7 in already.
2
That gets you to Player Level 2 Which unlocks the Weekly Guest Challenge — four random guests appear inside The Cat's Tail each week with a blue card icon over their heads. One win clears objective 3.
3
Buy the letter at 1,000 coins Clears objective 1 and completes The First Match. Then spend the letter on Skirk.
Coin rates to expect: open-world duels scale with Player Level — a few hundred coins each early, rising steeply (a Level 7–8 opponent pays around 1,500). Weekly guests pay roughly 300 at Player Level 2 and 600 at Level 4, across four guests a week. At Player Level 7, Serious Showdown versions of Invitationals and Tavern Challenges unlock at 1,000 coins each. Cap is 50,000 — don't hoard past it.

Letters are the bottleneck, not coins. Shop stock is gated by Player Level — 1 letter each at Levels 2, 3 and 4, then 5 at Level 5, and the shop only goes unlimited at Player Level 10. Levels 2–9 also hand you 8 free letters. So before Level 10 you have a hard cap on how many characters you can unlock, and which character you spend a letter on matters.
Spend your next letter on Skirk. She's the strongest thing you can add to a Cryo deck, and you already own her in the open world — the card plays nothing like the character, but it's an excellent card in its own right.

Void Rift
Discard 1 card from hand whose original cost is 3 dice → Skirk gains 2 Serpent's Subtlety.
Mutual Weapons Mentorship
Attaches Seven-Phase Flash: +1 DMG on its next instance. At round start, or after you switch characters, it's discarded and you gain 1 Serpent's Subtlety.
Talent card
Far To Fall — 1 Cryo, Fast Action. While equipped and she's on field, playing or discarding a Void Rift deals 1 Cryo DMG to the opposing active character. Once per round.
Read the Burst again — it's the whole deck. 7 Serpent's Subtlety means 7 Cryo damage to the active character and 3 piercing to each one behind it, for 3 dice. Everything in the deck exists to get her to 7 before you fire.

The trap: Void Rift needs a 3-cost card to throw away. Not any card — one whose original cost is 3 dice. If your deck is all cheap cards you will hold Void Rifts you can't use and Skirk never charges. Build in 6–8 three-cost cards as ammunition and treat them as fuel first, playable cards second.

5 · The deck

Skirk · Kaeya · Xingqiu. Two Invitation Letters — one for Skirk, one for Xingqiu. Kaeya is free from the tutorial.
Changed twice, 27 Oct — here's the trail. First I built it around Fischl (free, Oz auto-generates rifts). Then Mona, for the damage doubling. Then I went and looked up what the game itself does with Skirk — her Invitational deck is Skirk · Xingqiu · Escoffier, won through repeated Frozen. That's better evidence than my reasoning, so Xingqiu it is, with Kaeya in Escoffier's slot. Mona and Fischl both remain good; see below.
She does not have to tank the whole charge. Her passive fires when "your side" triggers the reaction, and Void Rift says "your allied Skirk" gains the Serpent's Subtlety — neither says active. So she banks from the back bench while Kaeya and your third character do the work. She only has to be on field for her Skill (+2) and the Burst — one or two turns of exposure, not six. Far To Fall is the exception: its bonus damage does require her on field.
The constraint that actually decides your third slot: it can't be Cryo. Skirk is Cryo and Kaeya is Cryo. Mono-Cryo triggers no reactions at all — no Frozen, no Superconduct, no Swirl, no Crystallize — which means zero Void Rifts and a Burst that never charges. The third character has to be Hydro, Electro, Anemo or Geo, or you must bench Kaeya.

What the research actually says

There is no published Skirk deck. I checked the main community deck aggregator — 34 top decks, not one contains her — and GameWith's Skirk card page lists no recommended teammates at all. She's too new for the meta lists to have caught up. Anything anyone tells you about "the best Skirk deck" right now, including me, is reasoning from her card text rather than quoting results.
The strongest evidence available is HoYoverse's own Skirk deck. Her Invitational stage — the one you'll be fighting — runs Skirk · Xingqiu · Escoffier, and the in-game hint describes it as building momentum by repeatedly inflicting Frozen and converting that control into one powerful final strike. That's the designers telling you the shell: Cryo carry + Cryo support + Hydro enabler, winning through Frozen.
So the pick is Xingqiu, not Mona. Escoffier isn't in your Player List, but Xingqiu is — and Kaeya covers Escoffier's slot for free. That makes your deck Skirk · Kaeya · Xingqiu: the official shell, one letter, built tonight.

Spending your letter

★
Xingqiu — the pick Raincutter leaves Rainbow Bladework: 1 Hydro DMG after each of your Normal Attacks, 3 uses, plus Hydro application on the character. Against Kaeya's Cryo that's Frozen over and over — and Frozen is the engine here. Every freeze is a Void Rift, and a frozen opponent loses its turn, which is also the answer to Skirk dying before she fires. Consistency is what this deck lacks, not ceiling: if the rifts don't arrive, Skirk never charges and no multiplier matters.
2
Mona — the higher ceiling, the lower floor Stellaris Phantasm (3 Hydro + 3 Energy) deals 4 Hydro DMG and leaves an Illusory Bubble: the next instance of Skill DMG is doubled, applied after every additive bonus. Skirk's 7-stack Burst becomes 14. She's also Hydro for Frozen, and her passive makes switching off her a Fast Action once per round — which matters in a deck that swaps constantly, since every switch also discards Mutual Weapons Mentorship for +1 Serpent's Subtlety.
3
Diona — the survivability pick, but it costs you Kaeya Cryo Bow, 12 HP. Icy Paws (3 Cryo) deals 2 Cryo and gives a Cat-Claw Shield; her Burst heals her 2 and summons Drunken Mist, which deals 1 Cryo and heals your active character 2 HP every End Phase — that's sustain while Skirk is the one standing out front. Her Talent is 3 dice, so it doubles as fuel, and being a Bow user makes Sacrificial Bow live again. But she's Cryo — so the lineup becomes Skirk · Diona · Fischl and Kaeya sits out, with Fischl's Oz carrying the Superconduct rifts alone.
✗
Tartaglia — a strong card, the wrong job Hydro, so he'd make Frozen, and his Talent is 3 dice so it doubles as fuel. Two real problems though. He's a Bow user — Skirk, Kaeya and Xingqiu are all swords, so he splits your weapon cards in half. And he wants the field: Foul Legacy puts him in Melee Stance for 2 rounds, his Charged Attacks apply Riptide, and his big Burst is the 7-damage melee one at 3 Energy. He's a carry, not an enabler — he and Skirk compete for field time and dice, and you end up doing neither plan well. His own shell is Tartaglia + Raiden + Beidou on Electro-Charged.
✗
Not Ganyu, Ayaka or Chongyun All fine Cryo cards, but they want to be the one dealing the damage — and a third Cryo makes the reaction problem worse. You already have a finisher; what you lack is a multiplier or a medic.

Frozen is defence This is the part that answers your worry directly: Mona's Hydro on Kaeya's Cryo freezes the opponent, and a frozen character loses its turn. A turn they don't get is damage you don't take — while also handing you a Void Rift.
Take Mona unless you're actually losing games to damage. She answers all three of your asks — fast switch built into her passive, Frozen denying the opponent turns, and she doubles the payoff. If you find Skirk dying before she fires even after adding the food cards, Diona is the right second letter and the deck becomes Skirk · Diona · Fischl.
One thing I haven't verified: whether Illusory Bubble also doubles the piercing damage Skirk's Burst sends to the standby characters, or only the main Cryo hit. The card text says "double the DMG dealt for this instance" — read it in game before you count on 6 piercing.
1
Skirk — the finisher Stays back, banks Serpent's Subtlety off everyone else's reactions, comes in and fires one enormous Burst.
2
Kaeya — the Cryo applicator This is the part people get wrong. Skirk's Normal Attack is Physical and her Skill deals no damage — she barely applies Cryo herself. Kaeya is what puts Cryo on the board so there's something to react with. He's also your second Cryo character, which unlocks the Cryo Elemental Resonance cards.
3

·
Fischl — the budget substitute Free from the tutorial. Oz pings Electro every End Phase on his own; Electro onto Kaeya's Cryo = Superconduct = a free rift every round with no card and no turn spent. Most reliable rift generator you can get without a letter — just no damage multiplier.
Why not Sucrose? Cryo Swirl also makes rifts, but she has to be on field and hit something that already has Cryo on it. Oz does it automatically. Diluc is the one to cut either way — Melt makes no Void Rift at all.

Food and healing Skirk is 10 HP and has to survive to the round she fires. Keep your usual 1–2 food cards.

Adeptus' Temptation is the card that breaks the game open. +3 Elemental Burst DMG is additive, and Mona's Illusory Bubble doubles after additive bonuses. So: 7 Serpent's Subtlety → +3 → 10 → doubled → 20 Cryo damage in one hit. Against a 10 HP card that's an overkill; against two it's a game.
Tuning the fuel. Six 3-cost cards is the floor. If you find yourself holding Void Rifts you can't discard into, cut the Northern Smoked Chicken and one Mondstadt Hash Brown for two more 3-cost weapons. If you're drowning in fuel and starving for plays, go the other way.

How it plays

1 · Kaeya applies Cryo 2 · Fischl drops Oz 3 · Superconduct → Void Rift 4 · Discard 3-cost → +2 SS 5 · Skirk in, Skill, Burst at 7
R1
Set the engine up Kaeya's Skill for Cryo, Xingqiu's Burst for Rainbow Bladework. Frozen triggers, you get a rift — and the opponent loses a turn. Skirk's Skill once for +2, and bank the Mutual Weapons Mentorship. (With Fischl instead: drop Oz and let Superconduct do the same job.)
R2
Bank to 7 Play up to three Void Rifts, discarding a 3-cost each time — +6. With the Skill and a Mentorship discard you are at the cap.
R3
Fire Switch to Skirk, Havoc: Ruin. 7 Cryo to the active character and 3 piercing to both characters behind it — that's 13 damage spread across their whole team from one card.
Don't fire early. The Burst is legal at 2 Serpent's Subtlety and it will tempt you. At 2 it does 2 damage and 2 piercing; at 7 it does 7 and 3. Same 3 dice. Always wait for 7 unless you're closing out a game that round.
