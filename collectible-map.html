Collectible maps

Every pin on the real map, grouped into warps rather than one long walking loop. Each numbered circle is a teleport; the lines show what it is closest to. Click a pin to tick it off, or a number to fly to that stop.

The route assumes you teleport. Walking order barely matters once you can warp, so the stops are ranked by how many pins each warp pays out — do them top-down and a half-finished run is still the best half. Within a stop, pins are ordered as a short walk out from the teleport.

Warps, best first

    What feeds what. Only Dragonspine's Frostbearing Tree takes a collectible you can map — Crimson Agate. The other offering trees take Sigils, which come from chests, Shrines of Depths and from offering oculi at a Statue of the Seven. So for Inazuma, Sumeru, Fontaine and Natlan the thing worth hunting is the region's oculus: Sacred Sakura via Electro Sigils, Tree of Dreams via Dendro Sigils, Fountain of Lucine via Hydro Sigils, Tablet of Tona via Pyro Sigils.
    Height, as far as the map knows it. There are no elevation figures in the data, so nothing can tell you a pin is "40m up a cliff". What it does record is whether a pin sits on a named floor — a cave, an underground level or a submerged one — which is the part that actually changes how you approach it. Those are drawn in purple and named on hover: Underground Lava River: Lower Level, Submerged Ruins, Rock Cavern Middle Level. A pin with no floor is open world, which may still mean the top of a mountain.
    Ticks are yours, kept in this browser. No API exposes which individual oculus or agate you have picked up — HoYoLAB publishes only a per-region count. So the dots start unticked and you mark them as you go; the counter tells you how many remain.
